# Writing order & per-section rules (Taylor & Francis "Writing your paper", distilled)

Order of writing: Tables/Figures → Literature review → Methods → Results → Discussion & Conclusion → Introduction → Abstract → Title.
Four A's before writing: Aims (what the paper is for), Audience (who reads this journal), Awareness (current debates/policy), Articulation (outline logic).

- Tables/figures: standalone, no duplication with text; split overcrowded tables; plan them before writing.
- Literature review: synthesize, interpret, evaluate strengths/weaknesses, expose the gap. Never a list of "X found Y".
- Methods: past tense, subsections; new methods described so others can replicate; existing methods brief + cited; name statistical tests and parameters.
- Results: findings only, no interpretation; ordered by importance, not chronology; secondary data → supplementary.
- Discussion (5 parts, in order): (1) principal findings at the interpretive level, not raw numbers; (2) comparison with prior work — similarities and why, differences and their source, where this work sits; contradictions are opportunities, explain them; (3) implications, theoretical and practical, strictly bounded by the data; (4) limitations, stated plainly, each tied to how it bounds interpretation; (5) future work derived directly from the limitations and open questions, specific not generic.
- Introduction answers in order: what is the problem; what solutions exist; which is best; what is its key limitation; what do we aim to achieve. Ends with explicit aim/contributions. Citations balanced (supporting and contrary), recent (≤10 yrs) plus seminal, and directly relevant.
- Abstract: 100–250 words (journal limit wins), standalone, no abbreviations/citations, never overstates the body. Structure: background (1 sentence gap) → aim → methods (design, data, measures) → key results with numbers → conclusion. Put the main finding and keywords in the first two sentences.
- Title: short, precise, informative; key keyword in the first ~65 characters; no abbreviations, formulas, numbers; ~10–12 words.
- Academic SEO: 1–2 keywords in title, 3–6 natural repeats in abstract, keywords in headings; never stuff.
- Editing: one main argument; state your angle; confident voice; drop defensive hedges; short sentences; one topic per paragraph; cut dead wood ("very unique"→"unique", "a large number of studies"→"many studies").
